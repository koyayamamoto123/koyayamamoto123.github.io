About

🧑‍🚀

I'm a PhD candidate in Aerospace Engineering at Texas A&M University (GPA 4.0/4.0), working at the intersection of control theory, state estimation, and trajectory optimization. My research focuses on making spacecraft maneuvers fast, accurate, and robust to uncertainty.

Much of my work centers on cislunar trajectory optimization and model-predictive control, high-precision numerical differentiation for dynamical systems, and estimation techniques that hold up in real, noisy environments. I care deeply about closing the gap between elegant theory and flight-ready software — including hardware-in-the-loop validation on real embedded platforms. These same guidance, navigation, and control methods carry over naturally beyond spacecraft — to drones, automotive, and robotics systems — and those are areas I'm especially excited to work in.

Before Texas A&M, I earned my BS in Mechanical Engineering from Waseda University in Tokyo, and I've since worked on navigation systems at VectorNav and spacecraft landing dynamics at JAXA/ISAS.
